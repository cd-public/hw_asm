<details>

`(!A & !B & C) |`

`(A & B & !C) |`
`A & B & C`

$$
(\neg A \land \neg B \land C) \lor (A \land \neg B \land \neg C) \lor (A \land B \land \neg C) \lor (A \land B \land C)
$$

## A Systematic Design Approach


1. Write the functional spec as a truth table
2. Write down a Boolean expression for every ‘1’ in the output
3. Wire up the ideal gates, replace them with equivalent realizable gates, call it a day, and go home!

## Pros

This approach will always give us logic

- It’s systematic!
- It works! expressions in a particular form:
- It’s easy!
- We are done! (?)

## Non-trivial Example

- Suppose
    - We have two unsigned integers with two bits of percision.
        - Perhaps a theoretical `uint2_t` (C) or `u2` (Rust) type
    - We wish to implement a comparison operator.
        - $A > B \implies Y = 1$
        - $A \leq B \implies Y = 0$

## Endianness

 - As a convenience, we take:
    - $X_0$ to be the "right" bit (the "low order" bit or least significant bit, the LSB) and $X_1$ to be the MSB.
    - Hello endianness my old friend.

## A note before

- Personally I'd use a decision tree.
